FINAL REASONS INSTEAD OF FINAL SITUATIONS -- what changed in grounding
=====================================================================
Branch: times. File: module/theory/basic_action_theory.pl (section 7,
SAFETY QUERIES). Measured on problems/problem3L with timing_profile.py.


1. How ProbLog handles the queries
----------------------------------
ProbLog does not ground "all queries at once". It:
  1. grounds the queries ONE AFTER ANOTHER. Each query(...) gets its
     own SLD proof search, but all of them write into ONE shared
     ground graph and ONE shared table (cache of already-solved goals);
  2. compiles that single graph once;
  3. evaluates every query's probability from the compiled graph.

The table means a sub-goal is solved only once: final_situation(S) is
computed by the first query that needs it (14.8 s for problem3L) and
every later query reads its answers from the table. But reading the
table is not free: a query still runs its OWN body once for EVERY
answer of the sub-goal it calls.


2. The final situations
-----------------------
final_situation(S) :- plan(Node), do_node(Node, s0, S, Outcome), ...

For problem3L this has 766 answers: one final situation per
combination of noise outcomes (z, zt, zbatt, sample draws). Each one is
a nested situation term 10-11 actions deep, ~550 characters, mostly
the control-point lists stored in startMoveto(...):

  do(checked(c4,...), do(haltMoveto(84.12, completed(a5), true),
    do(startMoveto([point(31.0,-5.0), ...10 points...], ...),
      do(planned(straight, completed(...)), ... s0)))))

In the ground graph each final situation is one AND node: the
conjunction of the probabilistic choices that lead to it.


3. BEFORE: every reason query inspected every final situation
-------------------------------------------------------------
any_reason_pattern(P) :-
    final_situation(S), halted_with_pattern(P, _ActionCode, S).

For each of the 28 reason queries (any_reason_pattern/1,
any_reason_pattern_by_action/2, any_reason_pattern_detail_by_action/2):
  - loop over all 766 final situations;
  - for each, call halted_with_pattern(P, _, S), which calls
    halted_with(Reason, S), which walks S from the last action back to
    s0 looking for a halting action (haltMoveto, planned, take_sample,
    ...) whose Reason matches P;
  - the query's ground node becomes an OR with one child per final
    situation where it matched (e.g. 766 children for
    any_reason_pattern(completed)).

Cost: ~0.95 s per query, x 28 queries = ~27 s. The walk itself was
cheap (mostly answered from the table). The cost was the ~16,000 calls
that each received the large term S: for every call the engine copies
S into the clause, checks whether it is ground, and hashes it for the
table -- all proportional to the size of S.


4. AFTER: summarise once, then query the summary
------------------------------------------------
  % every halting reason recorded in S, most recent first
  situation_reasons(s0, []).
  situation_reasons(do(A,S), [R|Rs]) :- halt_reason(A, R), situation_reasons(S, Rs).
  situation_reasons(do(A,S), Rs)     :- \+ halt_reason(A, _), situation_reasons(S, Rs).

  final_reasons(Rs) :- final_situation(S), situation_reasons(S, Rs).

  any_reason_pattern(P) :- final_reasons(Rs), reasons_pattern(P, _, Rs).
  (same for _by_action and _detail_by_action)

  - final_reasons/1 is computed ONCE (0.23 s) and tabled: every final
    situation is turned into its short list of reasons.
  - Many final situations end with the SAME list. ProbLog's table keeps
    one answer per distinct list, with a ground node that is the OR of
    all the final situations producing it. problem3L: 766 final
    situations -> 46 distinct reason lists.
  - Each reason query then loops over 46 short lists instead of 766
    situations, and never passes S into a call.

halt_reason/2 lists the halting actions (one fact per action functor,
indexed on the action), replacing the 12 clauses halted_with/2 used to
try at every step. halted_with/2, halted_with_pattern/3 and
halted_with_pattern_detail/3 still exist with the same meaning (now
built on situation_reasons/2), so their other callers are unaffected.


5. Effect on the ground graph
-----------------------------
                                   before          after
  ground nodes                     18,137          18,158 (+21 OR)
  edges                            46,890          46,288 (-602)
  atoms / AND nodes                1,314 / 16,144  unchanged
  derivation levels 2-11           --              unchanged

  Unchanged: the plan unrolling, the noise atoms, the 766 final-
  situation AND nodes and everything below them.
  New: a layer of OR nodes, one per distinct reason list grouping the
  final situations that share it (36 new; the other 10 lists come from
  a single situation and reuse its AND node). They appear at level 1,
  just under the query roots (1,092 -> 1,110 nodes at level 1).
  Changed: the roots of the 29 reason queries now point at the
  groups instead of at the final situations directly:

    any_reason_pattern(completed)            OR of 766 -> OR of 46
    any_reason_pattern(battery_depleted)     OR of 432 -> OR of 18
    any_reason_pattern(sample_success(..))   OR of 756 -> OR of 36
    any_reason_pattern_detail_by_action(sample_success(x,y,v,..),a3)
                                             OR of 42  -> OR of 2

Why the probabilities cannot change: "some final situation has reason
X" and "some group of final situations whose reason list contains X"
are the same Boolean formula -- the groups are just the final
situations partitioned by their reason list. Verified: all 128 query
probabilities are bit-identical to the baseline.


6. Timing (problem3L, grounding only)
-------------------------------------
  before   32.9 s
  after    18.7 s   (-43%)
  reason queries: ~0.95 s each -> ~0.05 s each

(After this change, ~71% of grounding is building the final situations
themselves -- do_node/poss and the collision checks. The collision
checks were then moved to a raster distance map in
module/theory/collision_geometry.py, bringing grounding to 14.5 s.)


7. Something to keep in mind
----------------------------
The number of distinct reason lists drives the cost of every reason
query. In problem3L it is 46 rather than ~5 because sample_success/
sample_failure carry continuous values (sample position x, y and
sample value), so each sampled position yields its own list. Coarser
reasons (e.g. dropping the coordinates, or collision(obsN) ->
collision) reduce the groups further, at the price of the queries no
longer distinguishing those values.
